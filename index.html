Maya's Night Before

Scenario 1 – One More Night

This is a short extract from a real branching video simulation – built exactly the way we've been talking about today. You can watch the scenes, explore all the response options, and follow any path through to Alex's feedback. It's a way to see how the simulation works from the inside.

What this is about

Maya has just accepted a job in content and brand work. Tomorrow is her first day. Tonight her friend Javi has shown up – and finds her getting ready to go out.

What this scenario teaches

Why the night before a first day matters. Why orientation is never just a formality. What the adjustment period of a new job actually involves.

You are playing Javi – Maya's oldest friend. He knows her well enough to know when she's kidding herself. You are joining at the second decision moment. Below is the context for what happened first.

The Opening Scene

Maya is getting ready to go out – the night before her first day at a new job. Javi has just shown up. Click the video to watch. Transcript available below if you need it.

Maya: "Oh good – you can help me decide what to wear. I've got a green top and a blue one laid out on the bed and I cannot make up my mind. Anyway – I'm meeting Priya and Sam later, it's nothing big, just drinks. Before you say anything – I know. First day tomorrow. But it's literally just a couple of hours, I'll be home by eleven, and it's not like I need to be sharp for the first day, right? It's just orientation. They're not going to throw me in the deep end on day one. Oh – and that thing on the table, that's the information pack they sent. Haven't had a chance to look at it yet. So – green top or blue?"

Now it's your turn

Maya is pushing back – and she has a point, or at least she thinks she does. How Javi responds next will shape whether she walks into tomorrow ready or not.

In the real simulation, a learner picks one response and follows that path to the end. They never see the others. That's why every path is designed to teach the same thing – just through a different conversation.
